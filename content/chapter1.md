# Introduction to automatic speech recognition

The ongoing digitalization of society has led to a rapid increase in the amount of digital data being created, collected, and stored. Alongside text, images and video, large volumes of audio data are now generated through interviews, meetings, lectures, podcasts, media content and voice-based technologies. Working with audio recordings can be challenging, as listening to and manually transcribing spoken content is often a time-consuming process. Automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems help address this challenge by automatically converting speech into written text.  

ASR, short for automatic speech recognition, is a field of artificial intelligence that focuses on enabling computers to recognize and transcribe human speech. In a speech-to-text workflow, an ASR system analyzes an audio recording, identifies the spoken words, and generates a written transcription of the speech. Modern ASR systems can process recordings in many languages and transform spoken content that can be analyzed, edited, shared, and used as research data.  

Speech-to-text refers to the process of automatically converting spoken language into written text. Instead of requiring a person to listen to an audio recording and manually type what was said, a speech-to-text system performs this task automatically. The ability to convert speech into text has become increasingly important in research, education, and business. By transforming audio content into a text format, speech recordings become easier to work with, store, and process using digital tools. Speech-to-text technology can also serve as the foundation of a wide range of other tasks, including text analysis, information retrieval, and language technology applications.  

 

## Introduction to Whisper 

This course focuses on Whisper, an open-source Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) model developed by OpenAI. Since its release, Whisper has become one of the most widely used speech-to-text models. Whisper is continuously updated and offers multiple different model variants for different purposes.  

Whisper is designed to convert spoken language into written text and currently supports 99 different languages. The model was trained on a large and diverse selection of audio recordings, enabling it to transcribe speech from different speakers, languages, and recording conditions. Whisper's different model sizes allow the user to choose a model that best matches their transcription needs.  

One of Whisper's key advantages is its flexibility. It can be used locally on personal computers, on webpages, and high-performance computing infrastructures like LUMI. As an open-source model, Whisper is freely available and can be integrated into different research and development workflows, making it an accessible platform for learning about speech-to-text technology.  

Throughout this course, you will learn how to use Whisper to transcribe audio data while gaining an understanding of the key concepts and processes involved in Automatic Speech Recognition.
